# PART 1: FREQUENCY CURVES

## 1. Frequency Curve

**Definition:** A frequency curve is a smooth curve that represents a frequency distribution.

**Roman Urdu:** Frequency polygon ko smooth karne se Frequency Curve banta hai.

## 2. Main Types of Frequency Curves

There are 4 main types:

### Type 1: Symmetrical Frequency Curve

**Definition:** A curve in which both sides are equal and have the same shape.

**Roman Urdu:** Iski left aur right sides same hoti hain.

### Type 2: Moderately Skewed Frequency Curve

**Definition:** A curve in which one side has a longer tail than the other side.

**Roman Urdu:** Is curve ki ek side ki tail doosri side se zyada lambi hoti hai.

**It has 2 types:**

**A. Positively Skewed Curve**

**Definition:** A curve with a longer tail on the right side.

**Roman Urdu:** Iski tail right side par lambi hoti hai.

**B. Negatively Skewed Curve**

**Definition:** A curve with a longer tail on the left side.

**Roman Urdu:** Iski tail left side par lambi hoti hai.

### Type 3: Extremely Skewed Frequency Curve

**Definition:** A curve in which frequencies are concentrated towards one end.

**Roman Urdu:** Is mein frequency zyada tar ek side par hoti hai.

**It has 2 types:**

**A. Extremely Negatively Skewed (J-Shaped)**

**Definition:** A curve that looks like the letter J, with high frequency towards the right end.

**Roman Urdu:** Iski shape J jaisi hoti hai aur frequency right end par zyada hoti hai.

**B. Extremely Positively Skewed (Reverse J-Shaped)**

**Definition:** A curve that looks like a reverse J, with high frequency towards the left end.

**Roman Urdu:** Iski shape ulte J jaisi hoti hai aur frequency left end par zyada hoti hai.

### Type 4: U-Shaped Frequency Curve

**Definition:** A curve with high frequency at both ends and low frequency in the middle.

**Roman Urdu:** Dono ends par frequency high aur middle mein low hoti hai.

## 3. Discrete Frequency Distribution

**Definition:** A frequency distribution that shows frequencies of separate and countable values.

**Roman Urdu:** Is mein countable values ki frequencies show hoti hain.

**It has 3 types:**

**A. Positively Skewed Distribution**

**Definition:** A distribution with a longer tail on the right side.

**B. Negatively Skewed Distribution**

**Definition:** A distribution with a longer tail on the left side.

**C. Symmetric Distribution**

**Definition:** A distribution in which both sides are approximately equal.

# PART 2: CUMULATIVE FREQUENCY

## 4. Cumulative Frequency (CF)

**Definition:** Cumulative frequency is the running total of frequencies.

**Roman Urdu:** Frequencies ko step by step add karte jana Cumulative Frequency kehlata hai.

**Formula:**

CF = Previous CF + Current Frequency

**Example:**

| Frequency (f) | Cumulative Frequency (CF) |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 5 | 11 |
| 3 | 14 |
| 6 | 20 |

**Calculation:**

- First CF = 2
- Second CF = 2 + 4 = 6
- Third CF = 6 + 5 = 11
- Fourth CF = 11 + 3 = 14
- Fifth CF = 14 + 6 = 20

**Remember:** Last CF = Total Frequency.

## 5. Less Than Cumulative Frequency

**Definition:** It shows the total number of observations less than each upper class boundary.

**Roman Urdu:** Har class ki upper boundary tak frequencies ko add karte hain.

**Example:**

| Class Interval | Frequency | Less Than CF |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10–19 | 2 | 2 |
| 20–29 | 4 | 6 |
| 30–39 | 5 | 11 |
| 40–49 | 3 | 14 |

**Remember:** Less Than CF mein frequencies ko upar se neeche add karte hain.

## 6. More Than Cumulative Frequency

**Definition:** It shows the total number of observations greater than or equal to each lower class boundary.

**Roman Urdu:** Is mein total frequency se shuru karte hain aur har class ki frequency subtract karte jate hain.

**Example:**

| Class Interval | Frequency | More Than CF |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10–19 | 2 | 14 |
| 20–29 | 4 | 12 |
| 30–39 | 5 | 8 |
| 40–49 | 3 | 3 |

**Calculation:**

- First = 14
- Second = 14 − 2 = 12
- Third = 12 − 4 = 8
- Fourth = 8 − 5 = 3

# PART 3: CUMULATIVE FREQUENCY GRAPH

## 7. Ogive

**Definition:** An ogive is a graph that represents cumulative frequency.

**Roman Urdu:** Cumulative frequency ko graph ki form mein show karna Ogive kehlata hai.

**Types of Ogive:**

### A. Less Than Ogive

**Definition:** A graph made using upper class boundaries and less than cumulative frequencies.

**Roman Urdu:** Is graph mein X-axis par upper boundaries aur Y-axis par Less Than CF hota hai.

### B. More Than Ogive

**Definition:** A graph made using lower class boundaries and more than cumulative frequencies.

**Roman Urdu:** Is graph mein X-axis par lower boundaries aur Y-axis par More Than CF hota hai.

**Remember:**

- Less Than Ogive = Upper boundaries + Less Than CF.
- More Than Ogive = Lower boundaries + More Than CF.

# PART 4: IMPORTANT NUMERICALS

## 8. Median of Grouped Data

**Definition:** Median is the middle value of a data set.

**Roman Urdu:** Median data ki beech wali value hoti hai.

**Formula:**

Median = l + (h/f) × (n/2 − c)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| l | Lower boundary of median class |
| h | Class width |
| f | Frequency of median class |
| n | Total frequency |
| c | CF before median class |

**Example:**

| Class | Frequency | CF |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10–19 | 4 | 4 |
| 20–29 | 6 | 10 |
| 30–39 | 8 | 18 |
| 40–49 | 2 | 20 |

**Step 1: Find n**

n = 4 + 6 + 8 + 2 = 20

**Step 2: Find n/2**

n/2 = 20/2 = 10

**Step 3: Find Median Class**

The first CF equal to or greater than 10 is 10.

Median Class = 20–29

**Step 4: Find Values**

- l = 19.5
- h = 10
- f = 6
- n/2 = 10
- c = 4

**Step 5: Apply Formula**

Median = 19.5 + (10/6) × (10 − 4)

Median = 19.5 + (10/6) × 6

Median = 19.5 + 10

**Median = 29.5**

# PART 5: IMPORTANT FORMULAS

| Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CF = Previous CF + Current f | Cumulative Frequency |
| Total Frequency = Σf | Total observations |
| n/2 | Median class finding |
| Median = l + (h/f)(n/2 − c) | Grouped Median |
| Range = Largest − Smallest | Range |
| h = Range / Number of Classes | Class Width |
| RF = f/N | Relative Frequency |
| Percentage = (f/N) × 100 | Percentage Frequency |
| Midpoint = (Lower Limit + Upper Limit)/2 | Class Midpoint |
